Final project for DATA301: which pairs of books are most strongly and frequently read
together, based on real reading patterns? I treated each Goodreads user's read list as
a "basket" and ran market-basket analysis (Apriori-style pair counting, plus confidence
and interest scores) to surface book pairs worth recommending to each other — book A's
readers frequently also read book B, so B becomes a candidate recommendation for A's
readers.

The dataset was too large to hold in memory as-is, so I converted the raw JSON into
Parquet and processed it in chunks with Dask, accumulating pair counts across chunks
rather than merging everything back into memory at once — an early attempt at the
latter reintroduced the exact RAM exhaustion I was trying to avoid. The top-ranked
pairs came back dominated by sequels and sagas (Twilight, The Hunger Games), which is a
reassuring sanity check: the method recovers genuinely related books, not noise. The
main lesson was scale-first design — chunking and incremental aggregation aren't
optimizations to add later, they're the difference between a pipeline that finishes and
one that gets killed by the OS.
