A nutrition tracker: log what you eat into a daily diary and see it against calorie and
macronutrient goals derived from your body and your goal. Mobile-first (iOS, Android)
with a web companion, from one Expo monorepo — `apps/mobile` for the Expo Router app,
`packages/core` for dependency-free domain logic (TDEE, macro targets, portion math,
ranking), and a Hono server over Postgres for the API.

The part I'm most deliberate about is refusing to fake precision: if a food has no
sourced cup measurement and no density category, Calora won't invent one rather than
guess a weight — a tablespoon of broccoli falls back to a density table and gets marked
`≈` because that number is inferred, not measured. The dev server runs Postgres
in-process (PGlite) so there's nothing to install to try it, and the food registry is
seeded from ~7,928 real USDA entries rather than placeholder data. Still early — the
core logging loop works end to end, but barcode scanning, recipe import, and plate
photos aren't built yet.

I've kept the verification side proportionate to a solo project: 159 tests and a
typecheck across all four packages on every change, a smoke test that asserts the
things that would otherwise fail silently (a cup of black beans really does resolve to
194g from a sourced portion, a food with no portion data is refused rather than
guessed), and a scripted browser run that drives onboarding and logging end to end and
screenshots each step. Design decisions live as ADRs in the repo rather than in my head
— including the ones that look like mistakes without the reasoning attached, like why
sign-in currently has no password.
